# Core Principles

## 1. Start With Expectation

Before writing, force three answers:

- Why are we writing this now?
- Who needs to read or hear it?
- Why do they care, and what should they do afterward?

The literal request often hides the real need. A request for "user portrait" may actually ask for business positioning. A request for "NPS" may actually ask whether the business understands user value and has a plan. Do not take the surface noun as the objective.

## 2. Treat Documents As Communication Products

The doc is not an archive of work. It is a communication product that must help the audience form the intended judgment with minimum effort.

- Demand-side view: organize by audience needs, decision logic, and business questions.
- Supply-side view: organize by team, project, or workstream. Use this mainly for internal checklists, operating reviews, or exhaustive inspections.

For leadership, cross-functional, or company-level communication, default to demand-side view.

## 3. Use Storyline Before Structure

Do not begin with a table of contents. Begin with the story the reader must understand.

A good storyline:

- States the key judgment.
- Explains why this judgment matters now.
- Shows the decision chain that should lead different readers to the same conclusion.
- Uses business logic as the main line and details as ammunition.

Test: if 100 qualified readers scan the storyline, most should infer the same decision direction.

## 4. Use Claim Headings

Headings should carry conclusions, not labels. Replace topic headings such as "Background", "Data", "Problems", "Plan" with claim headings such as "Growth is blocked by low conversion, not low traffic" or "The next constraint is governance capacity".

Scanning only the headings should reveal the main argument.

## 5. Build Logic Before Evidence

First decide the business logic:

- What is the current state?
- What changed or failed?
- Which factors drive the result?
- Which factor matters most now?
- What decision or action follows?

Then select evidence. Data, user research, cases, and anecdotes support the logic; they should not replace it.

## 6. Make Metrics Serve Judgment

Good metrics match the business chain and support a decision.

Use:

- Core result indicators plus process indicators.
- First-hand data where possible.
- Data with clear scope, denominator, time window, and comparison.
- Movement format such as `A -> B (+x%)`.

Avoid:

- Conceptual compound metrics.
- Data piles without a chosen core metric.
- Qualitative phrases such as "significant" without numbers.
- Attribution that only says one metric explains another metric.
- Second-hand data that silently imports someone else's decision logic.

## 7. Be Careful With Attribution

Only make causal claims when the business logic and evidence can carry them.

When uncertain, write:

- Known: what changed and by how much.
- Likely: what hypothesis is most plausible and why.
- Unknown: what cannot be concluded yet.
- Next: what will be checked, by whom, and by when.

Do not force external explanations just because they are convenient.

## 8. Drive Decisions Explicitly

Decision-oriented docs must name:

- Decision role: who can decide or commit resources.
- Options: what choices are available.
- Decision factors: what matters in choosing.
- Recommendation: what the writer believes should happen.
- Consequence: what happens if we choose or do not choose.
- Next action: owner and time.

If there is no option, no factor, or no owner, the doc is not yet decision-ready.

## 9. Describe Problems Without Over-Attributing

When reporting field research, offline walk-throughs, user interviews, or operations findings, separate observed facts from ownership judgment.

Use:

- Observed condition: what happened offline or in the user path.
- User impact: what uncertainty, cost, or failure it creates.
- Next action: what internal alignment or optimization will address it.

Avoid turning observations into premature blame. For example, write "线上可选驿站实际停业、地址误差、无人值守等线下经营情况，会增加用户寻站和成功寄件的不确定性" instead of "产品体验差" or "服务商问题严重" unless responsibility has been validated.

## 10. Build Consensus Into Reusable Principles

For key issue or discussion materials, the final output should not be only meeting notes. Convert discussion into:

- Stage consensus.
- Boundaries and definitions.
- Principles.
- Best practices.
- Exceptions or open issues.
- Owners and next checkpoints.

The highest-quality consensus docs let later teams apply the same principles without re-litigating the whole discussion.

## 11. Compress Expression

Every sentence should earn its space.

- Prefer conclusion first.
- Prefer subject-verb-object with a clear actor and action.
- Prefer quantified statements.
- Explain jargon at first use.
- Remove vague modifiers, filler phrases, and repeated context.
- One chart or table should tell one story.

## 12. Run A Contradiction Pass

Fluent wording must not hide incompatible logic. Before finalizing, test every major statement:

- **State and action:** Can the proposed action work in the stated condition?
- **Information availability:** Does the output rely on data the premise says is missing, stale, or unknown?
- **Scenario boundary:** Are normal and abnormal states, or mutually exclusive fulfillment stages, mixed together?
- **Time and capability:** Is a planned capability written as if it already exists?
- **Metric scope:** Is a category-level movement being presented as a total-business result?
- **Evidence and conclusion:** Does the evidence prove the claim, or only suggest a hypothesis?

Split conflicting scenarios instead of forcing them into one sentence. For example:

- Normal progress inquiry: show the current known node and predicted next arrival time.
- Tracking has not updated: state that the trajectory is stale or abnormal and provide an exception-handling path.

Do not write "tracking has not updated, so show the latest logistics node" unless another source actually provides a fresher node.
Do not make the contradiction disappear by dropping the stale-tracking scenario. Preserve every material user-provided scenario; split it, flag it as unresolved, or request the specific missing input needed to make the logic executable.
