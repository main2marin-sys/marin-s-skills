# Scenario Templates

## Business Progress Report

Use for biweekly/monthly reviews, leadership updates, business progress reports, OKR readouts, and cross-functional updates.

### Target

Let the audience quickly understand business health, main drivers, problems, decisions needed, and support required.

### Structure

1. **One-screen summary**
   - Current state versus target.
   - Main driver or blocker.
   - Biggest decision, risk, or support needed.

2. **Dimension 1: business mainline**
   - The 1-minute version: what changed, what matters, what it means.
   - Use the few indicators a business owner would check daily.

3. **Dimension 2: growth or operating formula**
   - Break down the result into the most explanatory factors.
   - Identify which factors are above expectation, stable, or below expectation.
   - Explain the business action behind the movement.

4. **Dimension 2.5: one deep dive**
   - Pick one case, anomaly, opportunity, or failure that reveals a system-level issue.
   - Use it to show a broader product, operation, organization, governance, or resource implication.

5. **Decision and next actions**
   - What we will continue, stop, strengthen, or change.
   - Required support, named owner, and timeline.

### Rules

- Do not report every workstream. Report the business.
- Do not over-drill into details most readers cannot act on.
- Highlight problem points and decision points; routine smooth progress can be compressed.

## Key Issue Or Consensus Material

Use for strategy questions, cross-team alignment, business rules, operating principles, playbooks, and materials intended to settle a disputed or ambiguous issue.

### Target

Align the key problem, converge on stage consensus, and turn the consensus into principles and best practices that guide later collaboration.

### Structure

1. **Expectation and question**
   - Who raised the question and why?
   - Who is affected?
   - What answer would be useful?
   - What will change after the answer exists?

2. **Problem definition**
   - What is the real problem behind the visible topic?
   - What is already agreed?
   - What remains unclear or disputed?

3. **Storyline**
   - Current state.
   - Core conflict or constraint.
   - Principles for judgment.
   - Recommended direction.

4. **Evidence and explanation**
   - Business logic, cases, data, user behavior, and operating examples.
   - Explain why the principle applies across cases; mark exceptions.

5. **Consensus output**
   - Values or principles.
   - Operating rules.
   - Best practices.
   - Exceptions and unresolved questions.
   - Owner and next review point.

### Rules

- Involve relevant stakeholders early; do not write in isolation.
- Use induction and deduction: form a hypothesis, test it against cases, revise, then abstract.
- A consensus doc is successful when others can apply it without the original author present.

## Storyline Or Speech

Use for leadership speeches, company or department presentations, strategy narratives, and materials where the speaker needs to create shared understanding before asking for alignment.

### Target

Establish common language, clarify the environment, and guide different listeners toward the same judgment.

### Prework

1. **Big environment**: what the audience cares about and what shared context already exists.
2. **Small environment**: what the speaker uniquely sees because of their role, position, and surrounding stakeholders.
3. **Goal**: what understanding, trust, or decision the speech should create.

### Structure

1. **Shared context**
   - Start from what the audience already cares about.
   - Clarify a familiar but fuzzy concept with simple definitions.

2. **New order or core judgment**
   - Explain the principle that resolves current ambiguity.
   - Show why this principle is necessary now.

3. **Classification or decision logic**
   - Define categories, boundaries, and key differences.
   - Make the audience able to classify future cases themselves.

4. **Capability or operating upgrade**
   - Translate the judgment into product, operation, governance, resource, or collaboration changes.

5. **Summary and call to action**
   - State what will be built or changed.
   - Name what each group should align on or do next.

### Rules

- The speaker's private objective should guide the storyline, but the visible text should guide the audience through decision logic.
- Use one image, table, or case to tell one story; avoid charts that exist only to display completeness.
- Build resonance from what key roles already care about.

## Discussion Material

Use for preparing business discussions, alignment meetings, issue workshops, and post-discussion conclusions.

### Target

Produce clear stage consensus, open questions, decisions, owners, and time points. The owner of the issue owns the conclusion, not a note-taker.

### Structure

1. **Before the meeting**
   - Clarify objective, stakeholders, expected answer, and use of the answer.
   - Share background material early.
   - Prepare a discussion storyline or frame.

2. **Opening**
   - State background and goal.
   - Name assumptions or hypotheses.
   - Define what is in scope and out of scope.

3. **Discussion design**
   - Use free discussion when there is existing shared context.
   - Use group discussion when broad participation matters and groups can be cleanly assigned.
   - Use written idea collection when the topic is fuzzy and needs more input.

4. **Time allocation**
   - Shared background and feedback: about 15%.
   - Collect and organize key issues: about 15%.
   - Decide what is truly worth discussing: about 20%.
   - Deep discussion and conclusion: about 50%.

5. **Conclusion**
   - Consensus.
   - Open issues.
   - To do, expected result, method, owner, and deadline.

### Rules

- Keep the discussion on the core storyline.
- Interrupt drift kindly but firmly.
- Use facts and data to cool down disagreement.
- Record unresolved disagreement as an explicit open issue.

## Metric Analysis

Use for KPI readouts, dashboard definitions, metric movement explanations, data interpretation, and metric-driven diagnosis.

### Target

Use metrics to understand business operation, validate whether the operating logic works, locate problems, and support decisions.

### Structure

1. **Business logic**
   - What user path, operating chain, or business formula does this metric reflect?
   - What result metric and process metrics matter?

2. **Metric selection**
   - Choose the smallest set of metrics that supports the judgment.
   - Include denominator, scope, time window, and comparison.
   - Prefer first-hand data.

3. **Movement description**
   - Use `A -> B (+x%)`.
   - Look at scale before quality or efficiency.
   - Separate result indicators from process indicators.

4. **Diagnosis**
   - List possible factors.
   - Test factors with matching measures.
   - Connect the movement to user behavior or business action.
   - Peel away factors that should not affect the result.

5. **Conclusion**
   - What changed.
   - What likely caused it.
   - Confidence level or uncertainty.
   - What action follows.

### Rules

- Do not use data to explain data; use business logic to explain data.
- Do not over-abstract metrics into concepts.
- Do not combine unrelated data into compound metrics.
- Do not force attribution when multiple factors changed.
- A dashboard succeeds when it solves most recurring business questions with a clear operating logic.
